1. 2026 AMC 8 Problem 12

Source & alternate solutions

In the figure below, each circle will be filled with a digit from 1 to 6. Each digit must appear exactly once. The sum of the digits in neighboring circles is shown in the box between them. What digit must be placed in the top circle?

Choose an answer for question 1

Worked solution

Focus first on the right side. The right-middle and bottom-right circles add to 4. Using distinct digits from 1 through 6, those two circles must contain 1 and 3.

If the right-middle circle were 3, then the sum of 6 above it would force the top circle to be 3 too. That repeats a digit, so it is impossible. Therefore the right-middle circle is 1 and the bottom-right circle is 3.

EdgeForced value
Top + 1 = 6Top = 5
5 + middle-left = 9Middle-left = 4
4 + bottom-left = 10Bottom-left = 6
6 + bottom-middle = 8Bottom-middle = 2
2 + 3 = 5The last edge checks

Answer: D — 5

2. 2001 AMC 8 Problem 14

Source & alternate solutions

Tyler has entered a buffet line in which he chooses one kind of meat, two different vegetables, and one dessert. If the order of food items is not important, how many different meals might he choose?

  • Meat: beef, chicken, pork
  • Vegetables: baked beans, corn, potatoes, tomatoes
  • Dessert: brownies, chocolate cake, chocolate pudding, ice cream
Choose an answer for question 2

Worked solution

There are 3 choices for meat and 4 choices for dessert. The two vegetables must be different, and their order does not matter, so the number of vegetable pairs is

C(4, 2) = (4 × 3) ÷ 2 = 6.

By the multiplication principle, the total number of meals is

3 × 6 × 4 = 72.

Answer: C — 72

3. 2024 AMC 8 Problem 2

Source & alternate solutions

What is the value of this expression in decimal form?

4411 + 11044 + 441100

Choose an answer for question 3

Worked solution

Simplify each fraction separately:

44 ÷ 11 = 4,   110 ÷ 44 = 2.5,   44 ÷ 1100 = 0.04.

Now add the decimal values:

4 + 2.5 + 0.04 = 6.54.

Answer: C — 6.54

4. 2020 AMC 8 Problem 21

Source & alternate solutions

A game board consists of 64 squares that alternate in color between black and white. The figure below shows square P in the bottom row and square Q in the top row. A marker is placed at P. A step consists of moving the marker onto one of the adjoining white squares in the row above. How many 7-step paths are there from P to Q? (The figure shows a sample path.)

Choose an answer for question 4

Worked solution

Ignoring the board's right edge for a moment, a path from P to Q must use 4 up-right moves and 3 up-left moves. Any choice of which 4 of the 7 steps go right determines a path, giving

C(7, 4) = 35 unrestricted paths.

Some of those paths step beyond the right edge. By the reflection principle, reflect each invalid path after its first step across that edge. The reflected path has 6 right moves and 1 left move, so the number of invalid paths is

C(7, 6) = 7.

The left edge cannot be reached from P in only three left moves. Therefore the number of legal paths is

35 − 7 = 28.

Pascal’s triangle method

We can also count paths directly on the board. Put a 1 at P. In each new row, write on every reachable white square the sum of the numbers on the two diagonally adjoining squares below it. If one of those squares lies beyond the board, treat its value as 0. This is Pascal’s triangle, clipped at the board’s edges.

5. 2015 AMC 8 Problem 4

Source & alternate solutions

The Centerville Middle School chess team consists of two boys and three girls. A photographer wants to take a picture of the team to appear in the local newspaper. She decides to have them sit in a row with a boy at each end and the three girls in the middle. How many such arrangements are possible?

Choose an answer for question 5

Worked solution

The two boys must occupy the two end seats. They can switch ends, so there are 2! = 2 ways to place them.

The three girls can be arranged in the three middle seats in 3! = 6 ways.

These choices are independent, so multiply:

2! × 3! = 2 × 6 = 12.

Answer: E — 12
