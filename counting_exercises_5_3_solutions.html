5.3.1

The Iberian trade commission

An Iberian trade commission has 8 Spaniards and 6 Portuguese. In how many ways can we form a subcommittee (to discuss olive oil trade) consisting of 3 Spaniards and 2 Portuguese?

Step-by-step
  1. Order does not matter inside a subcommittee, so each half is a combination, not a permutation.
  2. Choose the 3 Spaniards from 8: C(8,3) = (8 × 7 × 6) / (3 × 2 × 1) = 336 / 6 = 56
  3. Choose the 2 Portuguese from 6: C(6,2) = (6 × 5) / (2 × 1) = 30 / 2 = 15
  4. The two choices are independent, so multiply them: 56 × 15 = 840
Answer840

Watch out: a common slip is to add 56 + 15. Multiply — every one of the 56 Spanish trios can be paired with every one of the 15 Portuguese pairs.

5.3.2

Volleyball starters and the triplets

Our school's girls volleyball team has 14 players, including a set of 3 triplets: Alicia, Amanda and Anna. In how many ways can we choose 6 starters:

  1. with no restrictions?
  2. if all three triplets are in the starting lineup?
  3. if exactly one of the triplets is in the starting lineup?
  4. if at most one of the triplets is in the starting lineup?
Set-up

Split the roster once and reuse it: 3 triplets and 11 other players.

(a)No restrictions

  1. Any 6 of the 14 players, order irrelevant: C(14,6) = (14×13×12×11×10×9) / (6×5×4×3×2×1) = 2,162,160 / 720 = 3,003
Answer3,003

(b)All three triplets start

  1. Alicia, Amanda and Anna take 3 of the 6 spots — that part is forced, 1 way.
  2. Fill the remaining 3 spots from the 11 non-triplets: C(11,3) = (11 × 10 × 9) / (3 × 2 × 1) = 990 / 6 = 165
Answer165

(c)Exactly one triplet starts

  1. Pick which triplet plays: C(3,1) = 3 ways.
  2. The other 5 starters must all come from the 11 non-triplets: C(11,5) = (11×10×9×8×7) / (5×4×3×2×1) = 55,440 / 120 = 462
  3. Multiply: 3 × 462 = 1,386
Answer1,386

(d)At most one triplet starts

  1. "At most one" means zero triplets or exactly one — two disjoint cases, so add them.
  2. Zero triplets: all 6 starters from the 11 others. C(11,6) = C(11,5) = 462
  3. Exactly one triplet: 1,386 from part (c).
  4. Add: 462 + 1,386 = 1,848
Answer1,848
Check your work. The four cases by number of triplets must rebuild part (a): 0 triplets 462, 1 triplet 1,386, 2 triplets 3 × C(11,4) = 990, 3 triplets 165 — and 462 + 1,386 + 990 + 165 = 3,003.
5.3.3

Ten dogs, three groups, two fixed dogs

Suppose we want to divide the 10 dogs from Problem 5.5 into three groups, one with 3 dogs, one with 5 dogs, and one with 2 dogs. How many ways can we form the groups such that Biter is in the 3-dog group and Nipper is in the 5-dog group?

Step-by-step
  1. Place the two named dogs first — that is the whole point of the restriction. Biter fills one seat in the 3-dog group; Nipper fills one seat in the 5-dog group.
  2. That leaves 8 dogs for 2 + 4 + 2 remaining seats.
  3. Choose Biter's 2 companions from the 8: C(8,2) = (8 × 7) / (2 × 1) = 28
  4. From the 6 dogs left, choose Nipper's 4 companions: C(6,4) = C(6,2) = (6 × 5) / (2 × 1) = 15
  5. The final 2 dogs must form the 2-dog group — 1 way. Multiply: 28 × 15 × 1 = 420
Answer420

Why no division by a factorial here: the three groups have different sizes (3, 5, 2), so they are already distinguishable. There is no over-counting to cancel.

5.3.4

Descending numbers

We call a number a descending number if each digit is strictly smaller than the digit that comes before it. For example, 863 is a descending number. How many 3-digit descending numbers are there?

Step-by-step
  1. Strictly decreasing digits must all be different, so a descending number is really a set of 3 digits from {0, 1, …, 9}.
  2. Once the set is chosen, the order is forced — largest, middle, smallest. So each set gives exactly one descending number: {8,6,3} → 863.
  3. Counting descending numbers is therefore counting 3-element subsets of 10 digits: C(10,3) = (10 × 9 × 8) / (3 × 2 × 1) = 720 / 6 = 120
  4. No leading-zero cases need to be thrown out: the largest of the three digits comes first, and the largest of three distinct digits is at least 2.
Answer120

The idea worth keeping: when an arrangement is forced by the choice, choosing and arranging collapse into a single combination.

5.3.5 ★

Mountain numbers

We call a number a mountain number if its middle digit is larger than any other digit. For example, 284 is a mountain number. How many 3-digit mountain numbers are there?

Step-by-step
  1. Write the number as a b c. The condition is b > a and b > c, with a ≠ 0 (it is the leading digit). Note a and c may be equal — 121 is a mountain number.
  2. Build the number around the peak: fix the middle digit b first, then count the outer digits independently.
  3. For a given b: the left digit a can be 1, 2, …, b−1 → b−1 choices; the right digit c can be 0, 1, …, b−1 → b choices. count for peak b = (b − 1) × b
  4. The peak must be at least 2 (it needs a nonzero digit strictly below it on the left), so sum over b = 2 … 9:
peak b23456789total
(b−1)·b26122030425672240
2 + 6 + 12 + 20 + 30 + 42 + 56 + 72 = 240
Answer240

Why not just C(10,3)-style counting: repeats are allowed on the outside (121, 454), so a mountain number is not determined by a set of three distinct digits. Fixing the peak turns one hard count into eight easy ones.
