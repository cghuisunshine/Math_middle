Maximum Number of Principals

Each principal of Lincoln High School serves exactly one 3-year term. What is the maximum number of principals this school could have during an 8-year period?

(A) 2(B) 3 (C) 4(D) 5 (E) 8

Solution

At the beginning of the 8-year period, the first principal could be near the end of a term. This arrangement allows the largest possible number of principals to appear during the period.

  1. The first principal serves only a short time at the beginning of the 8-year period.
  2. The second principal then completes a 3-year term.
  3. The third principal completes another 3-year term.
  4. With less than 2 years remaining, a fourth principal can begin a term.

Five principals would require four changes of principal. Consecutive changes are 3 years apart, so the time from the first change to the fourth change would be at least:

3 × 3 = 9 years > 8 years

Therefore, five principals cannot appear during an 8-year period.

Answer: 4 principals, choice (C)
2 2000 AMC 8 · Problem 22

Whole Numbers That Do Not Contain the Digit 1

How many whole numbers between 1 and 1000 do not contain the digit 1?

(A) 512(B) 648 (C) 720(D) 728 (E) 800

Solution: Use Leading Zeros

Write every number from 0 through 999 in three-digit form. For example, write 7 as 007 and 52 as 052.

  1. The hundreds digit cannot be 1, so it has 9 choices: 0, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, or 9.
  2. The tens digit also has 9 choices.
  3. The units digit also has 9 choices.
9 × 9 × 9 = 9³ = 729

These 729 strings include 000, which represents 0. Because the interval starts at 1, subtract this one case. The number 1000 contains the digit 1, so it is not counted.

729 − 1 = 728
Answer: 728 numbers, choice (D)
Common pitfall: There is no need to count one-, two-, and three-digit numbers separately. Leading zeros combine the cases, but remember to remove 000.
3 1999 AMC 8 · Problem 15

Maximum Number of Additional License Plates

Bicycle license plates in Flatville each contain three letters. The first is chosen from the set {C, H, L, P, R}, the second from {A, I, O}, and the third from {D, M, N, T}. When Flatville needed more license plates, they added two new letters. The new letters may both be added to one set or one to another set. What is the largest possible number of ADDITIONAL license plates that can be made by adding two letters?

(A) 24(B) 30 (C) 36(D) 40 (E) 60

Step 1: Count the Original Plates

5 × 3 × 4 = 60

Step 2: Compare All Ways to Add the Letters

Location of the new lettersNew totalAdditional plates
Both in position 17×3×4 = 8424
Both in position 25×5×4 = 10040
Both in position 35×3×6 = 9030
One each in positions 1 and 26×4×4 = 9636
One each in positions 1 and 36×3×5 = 9030
One each in positions 2 and 35×4×5 = 10040

The largest new total is 100 plates, so the greatest possible number of additional plates is:

100 − 60 = 40
Answer: 40 additional plates, choice (D)
Key detail: The question asks for the number of additional plates, not the total number after adding the letters.
4 2001 AMC 8 · Problem 18

The Product of Two Dice Is a Multiple of 5

Two dice are thrown. What is the probability that the product of the two numbers is a multiple of 5?

(A) 1/36(B) 1/18 (C) 1/6(D) 11/36 (E) 1/3

Solution

A die can show only 1 through 6, and the only possible result containing a factor of 5 is 5. Therefore, the product is a multiple of 5 exactly when at least one die shows 5.

The complement is easiest to count:

  1. Two dice have 6×6 = 36 equally likely outcomes.
  2. Each die has 5 outcomes other than 5.
  3. There are 5×5 = 25 outcomes in which neither die shows 5.
  4. Therefore, 36−25 = 11 outcomes have at least one 5.
P = 1 − (5/6)² = 1 − 25/36 = 11/36
Answer: 11/36, choice (D)
Alternative count: There are 6 outcomes with the first die equal to 5 and 6 with the second die equal to 5. The outcome (5,5) is counted twice, so there are 6+6−1=11 favorable outcomes.
5 2012 AMC 8 · Problem 10

Four-Digit Numbers Using the Digits of 2012

How many 4-digit numbers greater than 1000 are there that use the four digits of 2012?

(A) 6(B) 7 (C) 8(D) 9 (E) 12

Step 1: Count All Distinct Arrangements

Two of the four digits are identical 2s, so the number of distinct arrangements is:

4! / 2! = 12

Step 2: Remove Arrangements Beginning with 0

If 0 is fixed in the first position, the remaining digits 2, 1, and 2 can be arranged in:

3! / 2! = 3

These arrangements represent three-digit numbers rather than four-digit numbers, so they must be removed.

12 − 3 = 9

Every remaining arrangement begins with 1 or 2, so each is a four-digit number greater than 1000.

Answer: 9 numbers, choice (D)
Common pitfall: The two 2s are identical, so they cannot be treated as different objects. Also, 0 cannot be the first digit of a four-digit number.
