12024 AMC 8 · Problem 25

A small airplane has 4 rows of seats with 3 seats in each row. Eight passengers have boarded the plane and are distributed randomly among the seats. A married couple is next to board. What is the probability there will be 2 adjacent seats in the same row for the couple?

  • A815
  • B3255
  • C2033
  • D3455
  • E811

Solution

Track the empty seats instead of the passengers. There are 12 seats and 8 passengers, so exactly 4 seats stay empty, and every one of the

C(12, 4) = 495

sets of four empty seats is equally likely. The couple can be seated exactly when some row contains two adjacent empty seats.

Count the bad cases instead

A row has three seats, left–middle–right, so the only non-adjacent pair inside a row is {left, right}. That means a single row can hold at most 2 empty seats without an adjacent pair, and there is only one way to do it.

Now split the 4 empty seats among the 4 rows, with no row taking 3 and no row taking an adjacent pair:

2, 2, 0, 0C(4, 2) = 61 × 16
2, 1, 1, 04 × C(3, 2) = 121 × 3 × 3108

Finish

P = 1 − 195495 = 300495 = 2033

22019 AMC 8 · Problem 19

In a tournament there are six teams that play each other twice. A team earns 3 points for a win, 1 point for a draw, and 0 points for a loss. After all the games have been played it turns out that the top three teams earned the same number of total points. What is the greatest possible number of total points for each of the top three teams?

  • A22
  • B23
  • C24
  • D26
  • E30

Solution

Call the top three teams A, B, C and the others D, E, F. Split every game by who plays in it:

  • Inside the top three: 2 × C(3, 2) = 6 games.
  • Top three vs. bottom three: 3 × 3 × 2 = 18 games.
  • Inside the bottom three: 6 games — these give the top three nothing.

Upper bound

A cross game can give the top three at most 3 points (a win). An internal game distributes 3 points if decisive and only 2 if drawn, so at most 3 as well. Hence the three teams together earn at most

18 × 3 + 6 × 3 = 54 + 18 = 72

and since their totals are equal, each earns at most 72 ÷ 3 = 24.

Achieving 24

Both inequalities must be tight, so A, B, C win all 18 cross games — 6 wins, 18 points each — and none of the 6 internal games may be a draw. Each team plays 4 internal games and needs 24 − 18 = 6 more points, i.e. exactly 2 wins and 2 losses. A cycle does it:

A beats B twice  ·  B beats C twice  ·  C beats A twice

Every top team finishes with 18 + 6 = 24 points, and the bottom three top out at 9 points, so the standings really do have these three on top.

Answer (C)  24
32026 AMC 8 · Problem 25

In an equiangular hexagon, all interior angles measure 120°. An example of such a hexagon with side lengths 2, 3, 1, 3, 2, and 2 is shown below, inscribed in equilateral triangle ABC. Consider all equiangular hexagons with positive integer side lengths that can be inscribed in △ABC, with all six vertices on the sides of the triangle. What is the total number of such hexagons? Hexagons that differ only by a rotation or a reflection are considered the same.

  • A4
  • B5
  • C6
  • D7
  • E8

Solution

1. Every such hexagon is a corner-cut

An equiangular hexagon is convex, so no line meets it in three vertices. With six vertices on three sides, each side of △ABC carries exactly two of them, and consecutive vertices on the same side are joined by a hexagon edge lying along that side. What is left over at each corner of the triangle is a small equilateral triangle. So the hexagon is determined by the three corner cuts

p at A,   q at B,   r at C

and, going around, its six sides are

p,  s−p−q,  q,  s−q−r,  r,  s−r−p

where s is the side length of the triangle.

2. Read s off the example

In the picture the cuts are p = 1, q = 2, r = 2, and the hexagon side lying on AB is 3. So s − 1 − 2 = 3, giving

s = 6

Checking the other two sides: 6 − 2 − 1 = 3 and 6 − 2 − 2 = 2, matching the figure.

3. Turn it into a counting condition

All six sides must be positive integers, so p, q, r are positive integers and each of 6−p−q, 6−q−r, 6−r−p is at least 1. Equivalently:

p, q, r ≥ 1   and   every pairwise sum ≤ 5

Rotating or reflecting the triangle permutes p, q, r in every possible way, so two triples give the same hexagon exactly when they are the same multiset. Count unordered triples with p ≤ q ≤ r, where the binding constraint is q + r ≤ 5:

Cuts {p, q, r}Hexagon side lengths
{1, 1, 1}1, 4, 1, 4, 1, 4
{1, 1, 2}1, 4, 1, 3, 2, 3
{1, 1, 3}1, 4, 1, 2, 3, 2
{1, 1, 4}1, 4, 1, 1, 4, 1
{1, 2, 2}1, 3, 2, 2, 2, 3
{1, 2, 3}1, 3, 2, 1, 3, 2
{2, 2, 2}2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2
{2, 2, 3}2, 2, 2, 1, 3, 1

Anything else fails: {1, 3, 3}, {1, 2, 4}, {2, 2, 4} and the rest all have a pairwise sum of at least 6, which would collapse a side to length 0.

Answer (E)  8
42014 AMC 8 · Problem 12

A magazine printed photos of three celebrities along with three photos of the celebrities as babies. The baby pictures did not identify the celebrities. Readers were asked to match each celebrity with the correct baby pictures. What is the probability that a reader guessing at random will match all three correctly?

  • A19
  • B16
  • C14
  • D13
  • E12

Solution

A guess is an assignment of the three baby photos to the three celebrities, i.e. a permutation of three objects. There are

3! = 3 × 2 × 1 = 6

equally likely guesses, and exactly one of them is entirely correct.

Sequentially: the first celebrity's baby photo is matched with probability 13, then the second with probability 12 from the two remaining, and the third is then forced. That gives 13 × 12 × 1 = 16.

Answer (B)  1/6
52022 AMC 8 · Problem 23

A △ or ◯ is placed in each of the nine squares in a 3-by-3 grid. Shown below is a sample configuration with three △s in a line. How many configurations will have three △s in a line and three ◯s in a line?

  • A39
  • B42
  • C78
  • D84
  • E96

Solution

1. The two lines must be two rows, or two columns

There are 8 lines: 3 rows, 3 columns, 2 diagonals. An all-△ line and an all-◯ line can share no square, so they must be disjoint. Checking the eight lines: a row and a column always cross, each diagonal crosses every row, every column, and the other diagonal. The only disjoint pairs are two rows or two columns.

A configuration cannot be of both kinds at once: an all-△ row meets every column, so no column could be all ◯. The two cases are mutually exclusive, and by symmetry they have the same count.

2. Count the “two rows” case

Describe the grid by its three rows; each row is one of 23 = 8 patterns, so there are 83 = 512 grids. We need at least one row equal to △△△ and at least one equal to ◯◯◯. By inclusion–exclusion, subtracting the grids missing one of them and adding back those missing both:

512 − 73 − 73 + 63 = 512 − 343 − 343 + 216 = 42

3. Double it

The column case contributes another 42, and nothing is counted twice:

42 + 42 = 84
Answer (D)  84
