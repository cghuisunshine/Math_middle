The problem

A small airplane has 4 rows of seats with 3 seats in each row. Eight passengers have boarded the plane and are distributed randomly among the seats. A married couple is next to board. What is the probability there will be 2 adjacent seats in the same row for the couple?

The argument in full

Everything the narration says, written down.

  1. Count empty seats, not passengers. Twelve seats, eight passengers, so exactly four seats stay empty. A seating arrangement matters to this problem only through which four seats are left, and every set of four is equally likely.
    C(12,4) = (12 × 11 × 10 × 9) / (4 × 3 × 2 × 1) = 11880 / 24 = 495
  2. Translate the condition. A row of three contains two neighbouring pairs — seats 1–2 and seats 2–3 — so there are 4 × 2 = 8 places the couple could sit. They fit exactly when both seats of one such pair are empty.
  3. Count the complement. Counting "at least one good row" directly means inclusion–exclusion over four overlapping rows. Counting the failures is easier: no row may contain two neighbouring empty seats.
    P(together) = 1 − (failures) / 495
  4. The rule inside one row. Of the 2³ = 8 ways a row can be empty, five are legal: nothing, any single seat (3 ways), or the two outer seats. Two neighbours or all three are out.
    Empties in a row0123
    Legal patterns1310
  5. Split the four empty seats across the four rows. No row may hold three, so the only splits are 2+2, 2+1+1 and 1+1+1+1.
  6. Case 2 + 2 + 0 + 0. Choose the two doubled rows; each is forced to its outer pair.
    C(4,2) × 1 × 1 = 6
  7. Case 2 + 1 + 1 + 0. Choose the doubled row (4), choose which two of the other three rows hold a single empty seat (C(3,2) = 3), then place each single seat freely (3 × 3).
    4 × 3 × 1 × 3 × 3 = 108
  8. Case 1 + 1 + 1 + 1. A lone empty seat never touches another, so each row chooses independently.
    3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 81
  9. Add, then subtract from 1.
    6 + 108 + 81 = 195 split-up patterns
    495 − 195 = 300 patterns where they sit together
    300 / 495 = 20 / 33 ≈ 0.606
Answer(C) 20/33

Two moves carry the whole problem: count the empty seats rather than the people, and count the failures rather than the successes.
