Lesson 18

Binomial Theorem

In this lesson, I will explain each idea, read every expression aloud, and then guide you through the practice answer.

Use combinations as coefficients in polynomial expansions.

Here is the main result.

Theorem.

Read the expression as: open parenthesis a plus b close parenthesis to the power of n equals the sum from k equals 0 to n of n choose k a to the power of n minus k b to the power of k.

Now let us see why this works.

Proof idea.

In open parenthesis a plus b close parenthesis to the power of n , we multiply n factors of open parenthesis a plus b close parenthesis . To get a to the power of n minus k b to the power of k , choose exactly k of the n factors to contribute b ; the rest contribute a . This can be done in n choose k ways.

Now let us work through an example.

Worked example.

Expand open parenthesis x plus y close parenthesis to the power of 4 .

Read the expression as: open parenthesis x plus y close parenthesis to the power of 4 equals x to the power of 4 plus 4x to the power of 3y plus 6x to the power of 2y to the power of 2 plus 4xy to the power of 3 plus y to the power of 4.

Here is an important point to watch for.

Common mistake.

Keep the exponents adding to n . In every term of open parenthesis a plus b close parenthesis to the power of n , the exponents of a and b add to n .

Now pause and try this practice problem yourself.

Practice.

Find the coefficient of x to the power of 3y to the power of 2 in open parenthesis x plus y close parenthesis to the power of 5 .

Let us check the answer.

We need 3 factors to contribute x and 2 factors to contribute y .

Read the expression as: 5 choose 2 equals 10.
