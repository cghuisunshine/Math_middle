Lesson 1

Counting Lists of Numbers

In this lesson, I will explain each idea, read every expression aloud, and then guide you through the practice answer.

Learn the inclusive counting formula and why the extra “+1” appears.

Let us start with the key idea.

Key idea.

To count all integers from a to b , inclusive:

Read the expression as: b minus a plus 1.

Now let us see why this works.

Proof.

Shift the list a,a plus 1,a plus 2, and so on ,b by subtracting a minus 1 from every term. It becomes 1,2,3, and so on ,b minus a plus 1 . Therefore the number of terms is b minus a plus 1 .

Now let us work through an example.

Worked example.

How many integers are from 7 to 29 inclusive?

Read the expression as: 29 minus 7 plus 1 equals 23.

There are 23 integers.

Here is an important point to watch for.

Common mistake.

Using b minus a gives the number of gaps, not the number of integers. From 7 to 29, there are 22 gaps but 23 numbers.

Now pause and try this practice problem yourself.

Practice.

How many integers are from 36 to 93 inclusive?

Let us check the answer.

Read the expression as: 93 minus 36 plus 1 equals 58.

That completes lesson 1. Review the key idea, then try the practice problem once more without looking at the answer.
