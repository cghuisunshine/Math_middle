Lesson 13

Complementary Probability

In this lesson, I will explain each idea, read every expression aloud, and then guide you through the practice answer.

“At least one” is often easier by counting “none.”

Let us start with the key idea.

Key idea.

Read the expression as: P open parenthesis A close parenthesis equals 1 minus P open parenthesis A complement close parenthesis.

Now let us see why this works.

Proof.

Exactly one of A and A complement must happen. Therefore P open parenthesis A close parenthesis plus P open parenthesis A complement close parenthesis equals 1 , so P open parenthesis A close parenthesis equals 1 minus P open parenthesis A complement close parenthesis .

Now let us work through an example.

Worked example.

Two dice are rolled. What is the probability of getting at least one 6?

Complement: no 6 on either die.

Read the expression as: 1 minus open parenthesis the fraction 5 over 6 close parenthesis to the power of 2 equals 1 minus the fraction 25 over 36 equals the fraction 11 over 36.

Here is an important point to watch for.

Signal words.

Consider complements when you see: at least one, not, none, or no more than.

Now pause and try this practice problem yourself.

Practice.

Three fair coins are flipped. What is the probability of getting at least one head?

Let us check the answer.

Complement: no heads, meaning all tails.

Read the expression as: 1 minus open parenthesis the fraction 1 over 2 close parenthesis to the power of 3 equals the fraction 7 over 8.
