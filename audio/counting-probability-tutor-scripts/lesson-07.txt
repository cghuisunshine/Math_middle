Lesson 7

Complementary Counting

In this lesson, I will explain each idea, read every expression aloud, and then guide you through the practice answer.

Count what you do not want, then subtract from the total.

Let us start with the key idea.

Key idea.

Read the expression as: wanted equals total minus unwanted.

This is often useful for “not,” “at least one,” or “no restrictions except...” problems.

Now let us see why this works.

Why it works.

Every object is either wanted or unwanted, and not both. So total equals wanted plus unwanted . Rearranging gives the formula.

Now let us work through an example.

Worked example.

How many 3-digit numbers are not multiples of 7?

There are 900 three-digit numbers. The multiples are 105 equals 15 times 7 through 994 equals 142 times 7 , so there are 142 minus 15 plus 1 equals 128 multiples.

Read the expression as: 900 minus 128 equals 772.

Here is an important point to watch for.

Common mistake.

Make sure the total universe is clear. Here the universe is all 3-digit numbers, not all positive integers.

Now pause and try this practice problem yourself.

Practice.

How many 4-digit PINs contain at least one 7? Digits may repeat, and a PIN may begin with 0.

Let us check the answer.

Total PINs: 10 to the power of 4 . PINs with no 7: 9 to the power of 4 .

Read the expression as: 10 to the power of 4 minus 9 to the power of 4 equals 10000 minus 6561 equals 3439.

That completes lesson 7. Review the key idea, then try the practice problem once more without looking at the answer.
