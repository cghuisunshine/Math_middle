Lesson 5

Partial Permutations

In this lesson, I will explain each idea, read every expression aloud, and then guide you through the practice answer.

Choose and arrange only part of a group.

Let us start with the key idea.

Key idea.

The number of ways to choose and arrange r objects from n different objects is:

Read the expression as: P open parenthesis n,r close parenthesis equals the fraction n factorial over open parenthesis n minus r close parenthesis factorial.

Now let us see why this works.

Proof.

The choices are n,n minus 1,n minus 2, and so on ,n minus r plus 1 . This product is the beginning of n factorial . Dividing by open parenthesis n minus r close parenthesis factorial removes the unused tail.

Now let us work through an example.

Worked example.

A club has 10 members. How many ways can it choose a president, vice-president, and treasurer?

Read the expression as: 10 times 9 times 8 equals 720.

Order matters because the jobs are different.

Here is an important point to watch for.

Common mistake.

Do not use combinations here. Choosing Alice as president and Ben as treasurer is different from choosing Ben as president and Alice as treasurer.

Now pause and try this practice problem yourself.

Practice.

How many 3-person officer teams can be formed from 12 people if the offices are president, secretary, and treasurer?

Let us check the answer.

Read the expression as: 12 times 11 times 10 equals 1320.

That completes lesson 5. Review the key idea, then try the practice problem once more without looking at the answer.
