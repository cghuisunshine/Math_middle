Lesson 6

Casework

In this lesson, I will explain each idea, read every expression aloud, and then guide you through the practice answer.

Split a problem into non-overlapping cases, count each case, then add.

Let us start with the key idea.

Key idea.

Casework works when every valid object belongs to exactly one case. Then:

Read the expression as: total equals case 1 plus case 2 plus and so on.

Now let us see why this works.

Why it works.

If cases are exclusive, no object is counted twice. If cases are complete, no object is missed. Therefore adding all case counts gives the total.

Now let us work through an example.

Worked example.

How many 3-digit numbers have exactly one zero?

Case 1: zero in the tens place: 9 times 1 times 9 equals 81 .

Case 2: zero in the ones place: 9 times 9 times 1 equals 81 .

Read the expression as: 81 plus 81 equals 162.

Here is an important point to watch for.

Common mistake.

Do not allow zero in the hundreds place. A 3-digit number cannot begin with 0.

Now pause and try this practice problem yourself.

Practice.

How many 3-digit numbers have exactly two even digits?

Let us check the answer.

Use cases for where the odd digit is. Even digits: 0,2,4,6,8 . Odd digits: 1,3,5,7,9 .

If the hundreds digit is odd: 5 times 5 times 5 equals 125 .

If the tens digit is odd: hundreds even but not 0 gives 4 choices, tens odd 5 choices, ones even 5 choices: 4 times 5 times 5 equals 100 .

If the ones digit is odd: similarly 4 times 5 times 5 equals 100 .

Read the expression as: 125 plus 100 plus 100 equals 325.

That completes lesson 6. Review the key idea, then try the practice problem once more without looking at the answer.
