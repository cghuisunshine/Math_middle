Lesson 9

Counting Pairs and Sums

In this lesson, I will explain each idea, read every expression aloud, and then guide you through the practice answer.

Pair-counting often leads to triangular numbers and the formula for 1 plus 2 plus and so on plus n .

Let us start with the key idea.

Key idea.

Read the expression as: 1 plus 2 plus 3 plus and so on plus n equals the fraction n open parenthesis n plus 1 close parenthesis over 2.

Now let us see why this works.

Proof.

Let S equals 1 plus 2 plus and so on plus n . Write it forward and backward:

Read the expression as: S equals 1 plus 2 plus and so on plus n S equals n plus open parenthesis n minus 1 close parenthesis plus and so on plus 1.

Adding gives 2S equals n open parenthesis n plus 1 close parenthesis , so S equals the fraction n open parenthesis n plus 1 close parenthesis over 2 .

Now let us work through an example.

Worked example.

In an 8-person round-robin tournament, every player plays every other player once. How many matches?

Read the expression as: 8 choose 2 equals the fraction 8 times 7 over 2 equals 28.

Here is an important point to watch for.

Common mistake.

If each of 8 players has 7 opponents, 8 times 7 equals 56 counts every match twice. Divide by 2.

Now pause and try this practice problem yourself.

Practice.

How many handshakes occur if 12 people each shake hands with every other person once?

Let us check the answer.

Read the expression as: 12 choose 2 equals the fraction 12 times 11 over 2 equals 66.
