Lesson 3

Multiplication Principle

In this lesson, I will explain each idea, read every expression aloud, and then guide you through the practice answer.

Count a sequence of choices by multiplying the number of options at each step.

Let us start with the key idea.

Key idea.

If choice A can be made in m ways and, after that, choice B can be made in n ways, then the two choices together can be made in mn ways.

Now let us see why this works.

Proof.

For each of the m choices of A, there are n choices of B. So the total is n plus n plus and so on plus n , with m copies of n , which equals mn .

Now let us work through an example.

Worked example.

You have 3 shirts and 4 pants. How many outfits?

Read the expression as: 3 times 4 equals 12.

Here is an important point to watch for.

Common mistake.

Do not multiply if you are choosing between separate cases. If the word “or” separates exclusive cases, you probably add.

Now pause and try this practice problem yourself.

Practice.

A lunch special offers 5 sandwiches, 3 drinks, and 2 desserts. How many complete lunches are possible?

Let us check the answer.

Read the expression as: 5 times 3 times 2 equals 30.

That completes lesson 3. Review the key idea, then try the practice problem once more without looking at the answer.
