Lesson 15

Expected Value

In this lesson, I will explain each idea, read every expression aloud, and then guide you through the practice answer.

Find the long-run average value of a random process.

Let us start with the key idea.

Key idea.

Read the expression as: E equals x sub 1p sub 1 plus x sub 2p sub 2 plus and so on plus x sub np sub n.

Now let us see why this works.

Why it works.

Over many repetitions, outcome x sub i occurs about a p sub i fraction of the time. Its contribution to the average is therefore about x sub ip sub i . Add all contributions.

Now let us work through an example.

Worked example.

You roll a fair die and win the number of dollars shown. What is the expected value?

Read the expression as: E equals the fraction 1 plus 2 plus 3 plus 4 plus 5 plus 6 over 6 equals the fraction 21 over 6 equals 3.5.

Here is an important point to watch for.

Common mistake.

Expected value does not mean the result must be possible. You can never roll 3.5, but the long-run average is 3.5.

Now pause and try this practice problem yourself.

Practice.

A game pays 10 dollars with probability the fraction 1 over 4 and 0 dollars otherwise. What is the expected payout?

Let us check the answer.

Read the expression as: 10 times the fraction 1 over 4 plus 0 times the fraction 3 over 4 equals 2.50.

The expected payout is 2 dollars and 50 cents.
