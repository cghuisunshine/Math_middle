Combination Review Problems

Extracted questions and step-by-step solutions · Problems 4.11–4.16

Main idea: Use a combination when order does not matter.
C(n,r) = (n choose r) = n! / [r!(n-r)!]
Useful shortcut: C(n,r) = C(n,n-r). Choose whichever lower number makes the calculation shorter.

4.12 Choosing books

Extracted question

Joe has one book each for algebra, geometry, history, psychology, Spanish, English, and physics in his locker. If he chooses three books, how many different sets of three books could he choose?

Step 1: Count the books: there are 7.

Step 2: He chooses 3, and the order of books in a set does not matter. Combination

Step 3: C(7,3) = (7×6×5)/(3×2×1) = 210/6 = 35.

Answer: 35 sets

4.13 Super Bowl party

Extracted question

I won a trip for four to the Super Bowl. I can bring three of my friends. I have 8 friends. In how many ways can I form my Super Bowl party?

Step 1: The winner is already included, so choose 3 friends from 8.

Step 2: The order in which the friends are chosen does not matter.

Step 3: C(8,3) = (8×7×6)/(3×2×1) = 336/6 = 56.

Answer: 56 parties

4.14 Lock buttons

Extracted question

A lock has 5 buttons numbered 1–5. The lock is opened by pushing two buttons simultaneously and then pushing one button alone. How many combinations are possible?

Step 1: Choose the first two buttons. Because they are pushed simultaneously, their order does not matter.

C(5,2) = (5×4)/(2×1) = 10 choices.

Step 2: For the single press, any of the 5 buttons can be used: 5 choices.

Step 3: Apply the multiplication principle: 10×5 = 50.

Answer: 50 combinations

This assumes the final single button may be one of the two buttons pressed first, which is the normal interpretation of the question.

4.15 Choosing four books

Extracted question

I want to read 4 books over the next month. My bookshelf has 12 books. In how many ways can I choose which books to read, without regard to the order that I read them?

Step 1: Choose 4 books from 12.

Step 2: “Without regard to order” tells us to use a combination.

Step 3: C(12,4) = (12×11×10×9)/(4×3×2×1) = 11,880/24 = 495.

Answer: 495 ways

4.16 Triangles in a dodecagon

Extracted question

How many triangles can be formed using the vertices of a regular dodecagon (a 12-sided polygon)?

Step 1: A dodecagon has 12 vertices.

Step 2: Any 3 different vertices form exactly one triangle. In a regular polygon, no three vertices are collinear.

Step 3: C(12,3) = (12×11×10)/(3×2×1) = 1320/6 = 220.

Answer: 220 triangles
