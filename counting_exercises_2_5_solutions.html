Exercises 2.5.1–2.5.5

Extracted questions with clear, step-by-step solutions using block counting, complementary counting, casework, and the multiplication principle.

Four ideas used on this page

  • Multiply when choices happen one after another.
  • Add when cases are mutually exclusive.
  • Make a block when objects must remain together.
  • Use a complement: total arrangements minus forbidden arrangements.

2.5.1 — Basketball team photograph

Extracted question. A basketball team has 5 forwards and 4 guards. In how many ways can the 9 players stand in a straight line for a photo if all 4 guards must stand next to one another?

Strategy: Treat the four guards as one block.

  1. Form one guard block.

    The line now contains the guard block plus the 5 individual forwards: 6 objects altogether.

  2. Arrange the 6 objects.
    6! = 720
  3. Arrange the guards inside their block.
    4! = 24
  4. Multiply the independent choices.
    6! × 4! = 720 × 24 = 17,280

Final answer: 17,280 arrangements.

2.5.2 — Happy Valley Kennel

Extracted question. The Happy Valley Kennel has 4 chickens, 2 dogs, and 5 cats. In how many ways can the 11 animals be placed in a row of 11 cages so that all animals of each type occupy adjacent cages?

Strategy: Make three blocks—one chicken block, one dog block, and one cat block.

  1. Arrange the three animal-type blocks.
    3! = 6
  2. Arrange the animals inside each block.
    Chickens: 4!    Dogs: 2!    Cats: 5!
  3. Multiply all independent arrangements.
    3! × 4! × 2! × 5!
    = 6 × 24 × 2 × 120 = 34,560

Final answer: 34,560 arrangements.

2.5.3 — The Jones family

Extracted question. The Jones family has 5 boys and 3 girls; 2 of the girls are twins. In how many ways can all 8 children sit in a row if the twins insist on sitting together and their other sister refuses to sit next to either twin?

Strategy: Make the twins one block, count all arrangements, and subtract those in which the other sister sits beside the twin block.

  1. Temporarily make the twins one block.

    We now arrange 7 objects: the twin block, the other sister, and 5 boys.

  2. Count all arrangements of those 7 objects.
    7! = 5,040
  3. Count the forbidden arrangements.

    If the other sister is next to the twin block, combine them into a larger unit. That larger unit can be ordered in 2 ways: sister–twins or twins–sister.

    Forbidden = 2 × 6! = 2 × 720 = 1,440
  4. Subtract the forbidden arrangements.
    7! − 2 × 6! = 5,040 − 1,440 = 3,600
  5. Arrange the two twins inside their block.

    The twins are distinct people, so they can switch places in 2! ways.

    2! × 3,600 = 2 × 3,600 = 7,200

Final answer: 7,200 seating arrangements.

2.5.4 — President and vice-president

Extracted question. A school MATHCOUNTS club has 20 members: 10 seventh-graders and 10 eighth-graders. In how many ways can two different members be chosen as president and vice-president under the stated conditions?

(a) No grade restriction

Choose the president first, then choose a different member as vice-president.

20 × 19 = 380

Answer: 380

(b) Different grades

There are two exclusive cases.

Grade 7 P, Grade 8 VP: 10 × 10 = 100
Grade 8 P, Grade 7 VP: 10 × 10 = 100
100 + 100 = 200

Answer: 200

(c) Same grade

Again, there are two exclusive cases.

Both Grade 7: 10 × 9 = 90
Both Grade 8: 10 × 9 = 90
90 + 90 = 180

Answer: 180

(d) How are they related?

Every pair is in exactly one of two cases: the officers are in the same grade or in different grades.

Part (a) = Part (b) + Part (c)
380 = 200 + 180

They form exhaustive, mutually exclusive cases.

2.5.5 — President, secretary, and treasurer

Extracted question. A club has 25 members and wishes to select a president, secretary, and treasurer. How many officer assignments are possible under each condition?

(a) Each member can hold at most one office

  1. Choose the president: 25 choices.
  2. Choose a different secretary: 24 choices.
  3. Choose a different treasurer: 23 choices.
25 × 24 × 23 = 13,800

Answer to (a): 13,800 assignments.

(b) A member may hold all three offices

There is no restriction linking the choices. Each of the three offices independently has 25 possible holders.

25 × 25 × 25 = 25³ = 15,625

Answer to (b): 15,625 assignments.

(c) A member may hold two offices, but not all three

Start with the unrestricted total from part (b), then remove the 25 forbidden outcomes in which one person holds every office.

25³ − 25 = 15,625 − 25 = 15,600

Check by casework:

All distinct + exactly two alike
= (25 × 24 × 23) + (3 × 25 × 24)
= 13,800 + 1,800 = 15,600

Answer to (c): 15,600 assignments.
