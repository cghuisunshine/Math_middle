1. Counting a cube

2003 AMC 8, Problem 1

Jamie counts the edges of a cube, Jimmy counts its corners, and Judy counts its faces. What is the sum of their three counts?

Solution

A cube has 12 edges, 8 corners (vertices), and 6 faces. Add the three counts:

12 + 8 + 6 = 26

Answer: 26

2. Basketball schedule

2005 AMC 8, Problem 14

A conference has two divisions of six teams. Each team plays every team in its own division twice and every team in the other division once. How many conference games are scheduled?

Solution

In one division, the number of pairs of teams is 6 choose 2, or 15. Each pair plays twice, so one division schedules 30 games. The two divisions therefore schedule 60 games internally.

For cross-division games, each of the 6 teams in one division plays all 6 teams in the other division, giving 36 games.

2 × (15 × 2) + 6 × 6 = 60 + 36 = 96

Answer: 96 (B)

3. Language books

2018 AMC 8, Problem 16

Nine different books consist of two Arabic, three German, and four Spanish books. How many arrangements keep the Arabic books together and the Spanish books together?

Solution

Treat the two Arabic books as one block and the four Spanish books as another block. Together with the three individual German books, this gives five objects to arrange, so there are 5! outside arrangements.

The Arabic books can be ordered inside their block in 2! ways, and the Spanish books in 4! ways.

5! × 2! × 4! = 120 × 2 × 24 = 5760

Answer: 5760 (C)

4. Product of two dice

2001 AMC 8, Problem 18

Two dice are thrown. What is the probability that the product of the two results is a multiple of 5?

Solution

The product is divisible by 5 exactly when at least one die shows 5. There are 6 outcomes with the first die equal to 5 and 6 with the second die equal to 5. The outcome (5, 5) appears in both groups, so subtract it once.

Favorable outcomes = 6 + 6 − 1 = 11
Probability = 11 / 36

Answer: 11/36 (D)

5. Three-digit products

2009 AMC 8, Problem 16

How many three-digit positive integers have digits whose product equals 24?

Solution

Because the product is nonzero, none of the digits can be zero. The possible unordered triples of digits are:

  • (1, 3, 8)
  • (1, 4, 6)
  • (2, 2, 6)
  • (2, 3, 4)

The first, second, and fourth triples contain three different digits and each have 3! = 6 arrangements. The triple (2, 2, 6) has 3 arrangements because the two 2s are identical.

6 + 6 + 3 + 6 = 21

Answer: 21 (D)
